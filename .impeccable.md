# Impeccable

## Design Context

### Users

Two audiences, opposite postures, one product.

**Parents** open the front desk on a phone, usually in a bad moment: 6:40am with a feverish toddler, or in a parking lot realizing lunch is still on the counter. They are anxious, one-handed, and short on time. They need an answer they can _trust_ well enough to act on — which means they need to see where it came from.

**Operators** (a center director like Ms. Rivera) are small business owners who are interrupted constantly. They live in a work tool between classrooms, on a laptop or a phone. They are not going to "train an AI" — but they will fix one wrong answer if it takes fifteen seconds and never comes back.

### Brand Personality

Composed, precise, quietly warm. The tone of a well-run front office: nothing decorative, nothing cold. It should feel like software that respects your time, not software that is excited to see you.

Three words: **calm, exact, unhurried.**

### Aesthetic Direction

References given by the user: **Linear**, **Intercom**, **OpenAI**.

What they actually share — and what to take from them:

- Dense information that never feels cramped, because spacing is systematic rather than uniform
- Near-neutral surfaces with a single restrained accent; color carries meaning, never decoration
- Small, confident type. 13–14px UI text, tight tracking on headings, high contrast for hierarchy instead of size jumps
- Hairline borders (1px, low contrast) doing the structural work that shadows would do in a lesser design
- Fast, small motion: 120–160ms, opacity and transform only. Nothing bounces.

Anti-reference, stated explicitly by the user: **must not feel "Claude-AI-made."** In practice that means avoiding the current build's soft sage/amber/cream palette, oversized rounded pills, chunky 16px+ body copy, and generous padding everywhere — the warm-but-generic look that reads as templated.

Theme: **light.** Derived from context, not habit — a parent checking a policy in a bright parking lot, and a director in a daylit classroom office. Dark mode would serve neither.

### Design Principles

1. **Citations are the product.** The source chip and the escalation card are the two moments that earn trust. They get the most design attention; everything else recedes to support them.
2. **Hierarchy through weight and space, not size.** Few type sizes, high contrast between them. Resist making things bigger to make them important.
3. **Color means something or it isn't there.** One neutral ramp plus semantic accents: teal for grounded/cited, amber for routed-to-human, coral for gaps. No decorative color.
4. **Density is respect.** Both users are busy. Show more per screen than a marketing page would, with tight grouping and generous separation between groups.
5. **Every interactive surface says so.** Pointer cursor, visible hover, real focus rings. Ambiguity about what is clickable is a bug.
